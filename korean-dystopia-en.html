New Release

Debut Album 《Korean Dystopia》

Release Oct 28, 2026 (upcoming) Label Mirrorball Music Tracks 12 main tracks Producers MOOII, Kim Jae-hyun

This album deals with events that took place in South Korea from the early to mid 2020s. Therefore, an understanding of the social context and atmosphere of South Korea during this period of time would be helpful if not necessary, and would make the listening experience altogether more enjoyable. If you lived in South Korea during this period of time, the lyrics will naturally make sense, but for the listeners who haven't had the privilege, we wish this page functions as a footnote to the album that makes what the album is getting at more clear.

The intention of this album is not to pass any value judgement or provide a deep reportage of certain social events. Rather, they are more or less a gathering of spontaneous thoughts, feelings, and observations we came to at the face of these events. There was an explicit effort at minimizing any form of revision or self-censorship when it came to the lyrics.

During the making of this album, Son Chang-hyun (vocalist) was a journalist, Kim Jae-hyun (Lead Guitarist) was a software developer, Jeong Min-gu (Drummer) was a part-timer (more commonly referred to as a "freeter"), Kim Da-mi (Bassist) was a university student, and Joon Sung Kim (former Bassist and continued collaborator) was a high school student. The album is to be a spontaneous and organic portrayal of society, based on the diverse Points-of-views that these five members brought to the table.

Our musical references include the Pixies, Pavement, Joy Division, and The Strokes, but after many-a-argument, for better or for worse, we failed to find a single, all-encompassing label that we could agree on sticking on the album. So on each song, everyone is playing what they want to play. Broadly speaking, Jeong Min-gu leans more towards Punk, Kim Jae-hyun towards post punk revival and shoegaze, bassists Joon Sung Kim and Kim Da-mi lean more towards post-punk, and vocalist Son Chang-hyun towards alternative and hard rock. We tried to recollect and list every song we referenced while writing each track.

Background

In 2023, MOOII was invited to perform at the Namwon Yoncheon Beach Festival and decided to write a song about summer. We placed an ad-hoc, working titled "Blue Hawaii" and finished the instrumentals and vocal melody, but the lyrics wouldn't come. The summer of 2023 was exceptionally hot and humid, and around that time there were a series of mass stabbing attacks that took place in Korea. After a discussion over a couple jugs of beer, Son Chang-hyun brought these lyrics with him. Some members objected to the sheer incendiary quality of these lyrics, but because the festival was supposed to happen the following week, the lyrics were approved. Throughout the past three years it's become one of our main repertoires.

Social Context

In the summer of 2023, a number of high-profile mass stabbings spread shockwaves across Korea. Choi Won-jong, one of the more notorious perpetrators of one of these attacks, who killed 2 individuals and injured 12 more, was the main inspiration for these lyrics. He was allegedly a heavy user of online communities with a pro-Japanese bent.

Background

A song making fun of people who think they're better because they made a bit of money in a bullish market, only to panic and convince themselves that they're on the right track after losing the same if not larger amount of money a couple of days later. Funnily enough, this is exactly what happened to us, so in a sense we're making fun of ourselves. Musically, the song came about when Jeong Min-gu, the drummer, watched a YouTube video titled "Ko Jong-soo vs. Koo Ja-cheol,"* and brought up the idea of starting the song in a similar way. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J5N-xcwjYUk

* Ko Jong-soo and Koo Ja-cheol are Korean football players.

Social Context

In Korea, a bull market driven by crypto in 2022 and more recently in 2026 by semiconductors drew in large numbers of young, inexperienced investors, and investing became the topic du jour, especially among men in their 20s. Among them, a certain mindset that you cannot get rich through your labor income and therefore you must invest as much money when you're as young as possible took hold. The older generation viewing this phenomenon critically is one of the many ways in which the generational divide and conflict in Korea is expressed. Today, almost all young office worker in their twenties or thirties own a stock portfolio of some form. But with prices so volatile, some have gained massive sums of money, while others have lost their life savings.

Background

A song based on the homonymous 2026 documentary "Pink Palace," an 80 minute long documentary detailing the sexual lives, desires, and sexualities of disabled people. Musically, the song was made in almost a glimpse of an eye: Jeong Min-gu heard Kim Jae-hyun trying out Michael Jackson's "Black or White" in the style of the Strokes, and asked him to make it more dreamy. On that same day, Oh Jun-myung was late to the rehearsal due to the Solidarity Against Disability Discrimination (SADD) protests in the subway, and used it as a motif for the song. Son Chang-hyun and Jeong Min-gu, both of them having been deeply impressed by the documentary, suggested the idea of naming the song "Pink Palace."

Social Context

From late 2021 to late 2023, Korea's Solidarity Against Disability Discrimination (SADD, 전장연) organized protests at major subways stations to demand a larger budget and investment for disabled people, especially when it came to public transportation and deinstitutionalization. The protests led to wide-scale disruptions in train services, and many people who felt that their lives had been inconvenienced by "disabled people" became hostile towards the agenda as a whole. Nonetheless, the protests became the cornerstone to the mandatory introduction of low-floor buses and a partial adoption of laws guaranteeing and supporting the rights of disabled people.

Background

A song that came about in a rehearsal the day after Son Chang-hyun broke up with his then girlfriend, born from an improvised jam. The original lyrics were pretty vulgar "You fucking con artist, bitch the amount of money I spent on you, You fucking ruined my life" to quote a few. However, after a thorough conversation with her, Son Chang-hyun, having reflected on his own missteps, changed the lyrics to a sort of confession. Below is what had more or less happened right after the song came together.

Kim Jae-hyun's thoughts: Son Chang-hyun's then girlfriend was an international student, and when her WeChat Pay account got suspended* and she was in a tough financial spot due to her tuition, he gave her about 5 million won no questions asked. After they broke up, the fact that he had lent her the money faded from his and everyone's memory. The total cost of Korean Dystopia was less than 5 million won. It's a lot of money**, and not paying it back did feel a bit like a form of fraud. I respect Chang-hyun for being cool about the fact that she never paid back, but I don't understand it.

* See "Social Context" below.

** About 3,724 USD as of October 13, 2026.

Social Context

A Chinese voice-phishing ring operating in Korea was caught after using WeChat Pay as a means of currency exchange. The Korean police force took the drastic measure of suspending every foreign-linked account that had attempted currency exchange via WeChat. Many people, especially tourists and international students including Chang-hyun's ex, who were exchanging money via WeChat were caught in the crossfire.

Background

Joon Sung Kim, MOOII's former bassist, skipped school (yes true story) to watch the impeachment trial of Yoon Suk-yeol, the former president of South Korea* live with a group of protestors. When his impeachment was ruled by the court, many right-wing protests where the impeachment was also being streamed, descended into chaos. Amidst the chaos, Joon Sung Kim slipped into the protests and recorded the reactions of the people with his phone. The "This country's ruined" in the middle of the song is from that very recording.

* Yoon Suk-yeol, the 20th president of South Korea, declared martial law in December 2024 and was impeached and removed from office in 2025.

Social Context

In Korea, voting tendencies are split by generation and sex: people in their 70s or older tend to vote right, those in their 40s to their 60s tend to vote liberal, and for those in their 30s and under, men tend to vote right and women tend to vote liberal to left. Behind this divide is a plethora of historical reasons from Korea's rapid economic development, a near universal male military conscription, a history of dictatorship, unemployment and more.
